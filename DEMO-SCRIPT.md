# DEMO SCRIPT — BudgetBallot (2:30–3:00)

**Recording target:** 2:30–3:00. Any longer and judges skim. Any shorter and we look thin.
**Recorder:** assume you have not read the code. Everything you need to say and click is below.
**Setup:** `npm run dev` in one terminal (Vite + API together), Chrome/Safari at http://localhost:5173, and a second terminal open at the repo root ready to run `npx vitest run`. Record at 1080p or higher.

---

## 0:00 – 0:20 — The hook (open on the allocator, slider under cursor)

**On screen:** allocator view, all sliders at baseline. Cursor already resting on the Public Transit slider.

**Action:** Slowly drag Public Transit up ~40%. On camera, three numbers update simultaneously — service outcome, equity score, emissions.

**Say (calmly, one line at a time):**

> "This is a $1.2 billion city budget. I just moved one slider. Watch what moved with it. Service outcome up 17 points. Equity up 4.2. Emissions down twelve and a half thousand tons."
>
> "Every other entry at this hackathon uses AI to decide for you. **BudgetBallot shows you why.**"

---

## 0:20 – 0:50 — The problem

**On screen:** brief cut to the Landing page's headline, then back to the allocator at baseline.

**Say:**

> "Cities move hundreds of millions of dollars every year across a dozen departments, and the reasoning almost never makes it to the people who live with the consequences. Residents get a bar chart in a PDF."
>
> "The theme for this hackathon is Smart Cities and Sustainability. Half of that theme — the sustainability half — usually gets left out of budget conversations entirely, because the tools to couple money and carbon in one place don't exist for regular people. BudgetBallot is that tool."

---

## 0:50 – 1:50 — Live demo (this is the meat)

### 0:50 – 1:10 — Allocate one intentional scenario

**Action:** Reset to baseline (button). Now build the "transit-first / green" case on camera:
1. Public Transit → about +40%
2. Efficiency Retrofits → about +70%
3. Road Expansion → about −40%

**Say:**

> "I'm going to make one intentional move — fund transit, fund efficiency retrofits, and cut road expansion. The kind of choice a climate-forward council would consider."

### 1:10 – 1:30 — Read the narrated explanation aloud

**Action:** Click "See full impact →" or navigate to the Impact tab. The plain-language paragraph is the first thing on the page.

**Say (read the paragraph from the screen — it will match what's below, generated live):**

> "This paragraph — right here at the top — is computed from the numbers, not generated by a language model. It cannot hallucinate. Same allocation, same words, every time."
>
> Then read the actual paragraph aloud, e.g.:
> _"You moved sixty-seven million dollars from Road Expansion into Public Transit. Public Transit rises from 61 to 79 — the largest single gain in this scenario. Equity improves by 4.2 points. Annual emissions fall by twelve thousand five hundred forty tons, driven almost entirely by Public Transit."_

### 1:30 – 1:40 — Show a factor breakdown

**Action:** Scroll to any service card. Click "Why this number? Show factors ↓".

**Say:**

> "Every impact number opens. Here's what actually produced it — baseline outcome, funding change, elasticity, and for emissions: baseline, funding ratio, direction, elasticity. No opaque coefficients."

### 1:40 – 1:50 — The Compare view (the trade-off table)

**Action:** Click Compare in the nav.

**Say:**

> "And this is the trade-off table. Your allocation against three reference scenarios. Roads-first pushes emissions up nine thousand tons and equity down. Austerity looks cheap and lowers emissions but wrecks service outcomes across the board. **The tension between money and climate becomes something you can read across a row.**"

## 1:50 – 2:20 — Technical: parity + tests

**Action:** Switch to the second terminal. Run `npx vitest run`. Let the green output show on camera.

**On screen:** `Test Files 4 passed (4) · Tests 51 passed (51)`

**Say:**

> "The impact model lives in two places — TypeScript in the browser for instant slider feedback, and JavaScript on the server for the API. That's a duplication risk. So there's a parity test that drives both implementations with the same inputs and fails loudly if they ever disagree. Fifty-one tests, all green. The parity test is our safety net when the math changes."

*(Optional 3-second beat: `code src/engine/parity.test.ts` — just show the file exists and it's short.)*

## 2:20 – 2:50 — Impact + scale + close

**On screen:** back to the Landing page.

**Say:**

> "Who does this help? Council members rehearsing a vote in the open. Civic organizations grounding advocacy in the same math the city uses. Classrooms teaching civics and climate literacy in a browser tab."
>
> "The engine is dataset-agnostic — any city can drop in its own budget and its own emissions factors and the same UI works."
>
> **"Most of what you'll see this weekend is going to be AI that decides for you. BudgetBallot is a tool that shows its work — every slider, every number, every factor. That's what a civic AI should look like."**

**End card:** Landing page with the tagline visible. Fade out.

## What NOT to say

- Don't say "AI-powered." We are the exact opposite. Say "computed," "transparent," "deterministic."
- Don't claim the emissions numbers are sourced. They're illustrative parameters (see README §Data provenance). If asked, say: _"the numbers are illustrative — the engine is designed to accept real municipal data without changes."_
- Don't demo the "save scenario" flow — it needs the write key and adds nothing visual.
